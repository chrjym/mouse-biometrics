## 1. SapiMouse: Mouse Dynamics-based User Authentication Using Deep Feature Learning (2021)

**Reference:** M. Antal, N. Fejér, and K. Búza, "SapiMouse: Mouse dynamics-based user authentication using deep feature learning," in *Proc. IEEE 15th Int. Symp. Applied Computational Intelligence and Informatics (SACI)*, 2021, pp. 61–66, doi: [10.1109/SACI51354.2021.9465583](https://doi.org/10.1109/SACI51354.2021.9465583).

**Summary:** The authors release the SapiMouse dataset for user-authentication and bot-detection research, describing its collection tools and protocol. Instead of handcrafted features, a convolutional neural network learns features directly from raw mouse data.

**Key findings:** The system reaches **0.94 AUC using only 15 seconds of mouse data**.

**Relevance to the study:** This shows that short mouse samples are enough to tell users apart, which supports the adviser's focus on short signals. Its features are learned, so it can't say *which* movement property separates users. The thesis answers that question with explicit geometric descriptors. SapiMouse is also a public dataset the thesis can benchmark on.

## 2. An Empirical Evaluation of Online Continuous Authentication and Anomaly Detection Using Mouse Clickstream Data Analysis (2021)

**Reference:** S. Almalki, N. Assery, and K. Roy, "An empirical evaluation of online continuous authentication and anomaly detection using mouse clickstream data analysis," *Applied Sciences*, vol. 11, no. 13, Art. 6083, 2021, doi: [10.3390/app11136083](https://doi.org/10.3390/app11136083).

**Summary:** The study collected mouse data from 20 participants and extracted about 87 features. It then compared decision tree, KNN, random forest, and CNN classifiers for continuous authentication and anomaly detection. Users were identified in three scenarios: (A) a single mouse-movement action, (B) a single point-and-click action, and (C) a set of both.

**Key findings:** Every classifier separated genuine users from impostors with comparatively high accuracy, including in the single-action scenarios.

**Relevance to the study:** This is direct evidence that **one movement or one point-and-click action** carries identifying information. That matches the thesis's choice of a single start-to-end stroke as the unit of analysis. Almalki et al. summarize each action into a fixed feature vector, whereas the thesis compares the stroke's shape as a whole.

## 3. Continuous User Authentication Using Mouse Dynamics, Machine Learning, and Minecraft (2021)

**Reference:** N. Siddiqui, R. Dave, and N. Seliya, "Continuous user authentication using mouse dynamics, machine learning, and Minecraft," in *Proc. Int. Conf. Electrical, Computer and Energy Technologies (ICECET)*, 2021, pp. 1–6, doi: [10.1109/ICECET52533.2021.9698532](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICECET52533.2021.9698532).

**Summary:** The authors argue that collecting data in "dull, administrative" settings, as in the Balabit dataset, may make mouse behavior look more uniform across users than it really is. They gathered a new dataset from 10 users playing Minecraft and trained a binary random-forest classifier per user.

**Key findings:** One evaluation scenario reached **92% average accuracy**, outperforming earlier work. The other reduced false acceptance of impostors.

**Relevance to the study:** This highlights that the **collection context** shapes mouse behavior, which bears on how the thesis designs its enrollment and test sessions. The per-user binary classifier needs impostor data at training time. The thesis's template-and-threshold matching needs only the genuine user's own strokes.

## 4. Detection of Advanced Web Bots by Combining Web Logs with Mouse Behavioural Biometrics (2021)

**Reference:** C. Iliou, T. Kostoulas, T. Tsikrika, V. Katos, S. Vrochidis, and I. Kompatsiaris, "Detection of advanced web bots by combining web logs with mouse behavioural biometrics," *Digital Threats: Research and Practice*, vol. 2, no. 3, pp. 1–26, 2021, doi: [10.1145/3447815](https://doi.org/10.1145/3447815).

**Summary:** The paper proposes a two-module detection framework: one module uses web logs, the other uses mouse movements. It combines them to capture both the **temporal** and the **spatial** characteristics of mouse movement. It was tested against moderate bots and against advanced bots that mimic human behavior.

**Key findings:** Combining web logs with mouse movements detects advanced, humanlike bots more robustly than either source alone.

**Relevance to the study:** The paper treats the spatial character of a trajectory as evidence separate from its timing. The thesis builds on this by measuring spatial shape (curvature, chord deviation, straightness, convex hull) directly. It also shows that mouse biometrics can flag automated input injection, which is one of the threats the thesis names.

## 5. Machine and Deep Learning Applications to Mouse Dynamics for Continuous User Authentication (2022)

**Reference:** N. Siddiqui, R. Dave, M. Vanamala, and N. Seliya, "Machine and deep learning applications to mouse dynamics for continuous user authentication," *Machine Learning and Knowledge Extraction*, vol. 4, no. 2, pp. 502–518, 2022, doi: [10.3390/make4020023](https://doi.org/10.3390/make4020023).

**Summary:** Extending their 2021 work, the authors evaluate three machine-learning and three deep-learning algorithms on a 40-user dataset. They cover both binary authentication and multi-class identification.

**Key findings:** For authentication, a **1D-CNN** performed best, with a peak average test accuracy of **85.73%** on the top 10 users. For multi-class identification, an artificial neural network reached **92.48%**.

**Relevance to the study:** This is a recent baseline showing what deep models achieve and how much accuracy varies between users. Those accuracies come from black-box models. The thesis aims for decisions that trace back to specific geometric properties of a stroke.

## 6. BeCAPTCHA-Mouse: Synthetic Mouse Trajectories and Improved Bot Detection (2022)

**Reference:** A. Acien, A. Morales, J. Fierrez, and R. Vera-Rodríguez, "BeCAPTCHA-Mouse: Synthetic mouse trajectories and improved bot detection," *Pattern Recognition*, vol. 127, Art. 108643, 2022, doi: [10.1016/j.patcog.2022.108643](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patcog.2022.108643).

**Summary:** BeCAPTCHA-Mouse is a bot detector based on **neuromotor modeling** of mouse dynamics. It is trained on human data and on synthetic bot trajectories made two ways: with heuristic functions and with a GAN. The authors also release a benchmark of 10,000 trajectories that includes real data from 58 users.

**Key findings:** It detects highly realistic bot trajectories with **93% average accuracy using only one mouse trajectory**. Fusing it with standard mouse-dynamics features improves accuracy by more than 36% in relative terms.

**Relevance to the study:** It shows that a **single trajectory** carries enough neuromotor information to separate human from machine, which supports the thesis's per-stroke design. The benchmark can serve as a source of synthetic "impostor" strokes.

## 7. User Authentication Method Based on Keystroke Dynamics and Mouse Dynamics with Scene-Irrelated Features in Hybrid Scenes (2022)

**Reference:** X. Wang, Y. Shi, K. Zheng, Y. Zhang, W. Hong, and S. Cao, "User authentication method based on keystroke dynamics and mouse dynamics with scene-irrelated features in hybrid scenes," *Sensors*, vol. 22, no. 17, Art. 6627, 2022, doi: [10.3390/s22176627](https://doi.org/10.3390/s22176627).

**Summary:** A user's behavior shifts when they move between tasks or "scenes," which makes authentication harder. The proposed method, SIURUA, picks features that correlate weakly with the scene ("scene-irrelated") and fuses them with user-related features.

**Key findings:** In hybrid-scene experiments the method improved authentication accuracy, reaching **84%**.

**Relevance to the study:** It shows that some features stay stable across applications while others don't. That is a criterion the thesis's descriptors should meet: a user's curvature and straightness habits need to hold across desktop tasks. It also flags task variation as a threat to validity for the experiments.

## 8. Continuous and Silent User Authentication Through Mouse Dynamics and Explainable Deep Learning (2022)

**Reference:** G. Ciaramella, S. Fagnano, G. Iadarola, F. Martinelli, F. Mercaldo, and A. Santone, "Continuous and silent user authentication through mouse dynamics and explainable deep learning," in *Proc. 21st IEEE Int. Conf. Machine Learning and Applications (ICMLA)*, 2022, pp. 1791–1798, doi: [10.1109/ICMLA55696.2022.00274](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICMLA55696.2022.00274).

**Summary:** Mouse dynamics are turned into four kinds of images and classified with several deep-learning models. **Grad-CAM** then highlights the image regions that drove each decision.

**Key findings:** The approach gives promising user-behavior detection results, and Grad-CAM offers visual explanations of the model's choices.

**Relevance to the study:** The authors recognize that **explainability** matters in mouse-based authentication. Grad-CAM only gives an after-the-fact heat map over a rendered image, though. The thesis builds interpretability in from the start: each score comes from named, closed-form quantities computed on raw coordinates.

## 9. ReMouse Dataset: On the Efficacy of Measuring the Similarity of Human-Generated Trajectories for the Detection of Session-Replay Bots (2023)

**Reference:** S. Sadeghpour and N. Vlajic, "ReMouse dataset: On the efficacy of measuring the similarity of human-generated trajectories for the detection of session-replay bots," *Journal of Cybersecurity and Privacy*, vol. 3, no. 1, pp. 95–117, 2023, doi: [10.3390/jcp3010007](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcp3010007).

**Summary:** The authors release ReMouse, a public mouse dataset collected in a guided setting. Unlike other datasets, it contains **repeat sessions by the same users**. They analyze it with statistical and machine-learning methods, including deep and unsupervised neural learning, to measure how similar the trajectories are.

**Key findings:** Two different users **do not** produce the same or similar-looking sessions on the same task. Even repeat sessions by the same user are distinguishable from one another.

**Relevance to the study:** This is the study closest to the thesis's matching problem. It quantifies how different trajectories are between users and between repeats of one user. The thesis's threshold must sit between those two kinds of variation. The finding that a user's own repeats also differ is why the thesis uses a calibrated threshold rather than an exact-match rule.

## 10. User Authentication by Fusion of Mouse Dynamics and Widget Interactions: Two Experiments with PayPal and Facebook (2023)

**Reference:** S. Khan and D. Hou, "User authentication by fusion of mouse dynamics and widget interactions: Two experiments with PayPal and Facebook," in *Proc. IEEE 20th Consumer Communications & Networking Conf. (CCNC)*, 2023, pp. 248–254, doi: [10.1109/CCNC51644.2023.10059968](https://doi.org/10.1109/CCNC51644.2023.10059968).

**Summary:** The authors fuse **a single mouse trajectory** with the widget interactions that happen during it (clicks, hovers), using score-level fusion. They test this on PayPal (10 users, about 12 minutes each, a short-interaction setting) and Facebook (15 users, about 2 hours each).

**Key findings:** On single trajectories, mean EER was 7.64% (SVM-RBF) and 3.25% (GBM) for PayPal, and 5.49% and 2.54% for Facebook. Combining the scores of consecutive trajectories reached **0% mean EER after 11 decision scores** on both platforms.

**Relevance to the study:** This shows two things the thesis relies on. One trajectory gives a usable but imperfect decision, and **accumulating evidence across consecutive strokes** quickly makes decisions reliable. That is the rationale for the thesis's per-action dynamic trust score.

## 11. Attacking Mouse Dynamics Authentication Using Novel Wasserstein Conditional DCGAN (2023)

**Reference:** A. Roy, K. Wong, and R. C.-W. Phan, "Attacking mouse dynamics authentication using novel Wasserstein conditional DCGAN," *IEEE Transactions on Information Forensics and Security*, vol. 18, pp. 3622–3631, 2023, doi: [10.1109/TIFS.2023.3245418](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIFS.2023.3245418).

**Summary:** The authors probe the limits of mouse-dynamics authentication by forging a legitimate user's action sequences. Their generative model, WC-DCGAN, produces fake action sequences accurate enough to fool a target classifier. They then analyze why the classifier was fooled.

**Key findings:** The generated sequences get a fraudster accepted as the legitimate user. Based on their analysis of the misclassifications, the authors propose a stricter mouse-dynamics strategy that significantly reduces them.

**Relevance to the study:** This defines a realistic **spoofing threat model** that the thesis's evaluation should cover. Because the thesis's decisions are interpretable, it can report *which* geometric property a forged stroke failed to reproduce. An opaque classifier can't do that.

## 12. On the Improvements of Mouse Dynamics Based Continuous User Authentication (2023)

**Reference:** H. Durmaz and M. Keskinöz, "On the improvements of mouse dynamics based continuous user authentication," in *Proc. IEEE 24th Int. Conf. Information Reuse and Integration for Data Science (IRI)*, 2023, pp. 13–19, doi: [10.1109/IRI58017.2023.00010](https://doi.org/10.1109/IRI58017.2023.00010).

**Summary:** Continuous authentication is framed as a binary classification task. The authors use residual learning (a ResNet-style CNN), a realistic data-augmentation method, and an improved way of mapping mouse data into images.

**Key findings:** Average false accept rate was **0.45** and false reject rate **0.34**, reported as **6.5 times better** than the reference studies.

**Relevance to the study:** This represents the current image-plus-CNN approach. Its gains rely on augmentation and a better image mapping, not on modeling user motor behavior. The thesis can use it as a performance reference while arguing for a transparent alternative.

## 13. User Authentication and Identity Inconsistency Detection via Mouse-Trajectory Similarity Measurement (2023)

**Reference:** R. Jin, Y. Liao, and P. Zhou, "User authentication and identity inconsistency detection via mouse-trajectory similarity measurement," *arXiv preprint* arXiv:2312.10273, 2023, doi: [10.48550/arXiv.2312.10273](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2312.10273).

**Summary:** The authors recast CAPTCHA-farm detection as **identity-inconsistency detection**. An embedding model measures how similar a session's mouse trajectories are to those recorded at registration or while the CAPTCHA was solved. A single model serves all users instead of one classifier per user.

**Key findings:** It outperforms state-of-the-art time-series classifiers, with **94.3% AUC** for identity inconsistency and **97.7% AUC** for authentication inconsistency.

**Relevance to the study:** This is the nearest match to the thesis's *comparison* method: a new trajectory is scored by its **similarity to an enrolled sample**, not by a per-user classifier. The difference is the similarity measure. Here it is a learned embedding distance. The thesis uses the discrete Fréchet distance, which is deterministic, works on strokes of any length, and can be read geometrically. Note that this is a preprint and has not been peer-reviewed.

## 14. Mouse Dynamics Behavioral Biometrics: A Survey (2024)

**Reference:** S. Khan, C. Devlen, M. Manno, and D. Hou, "Mouse dynamics behavioral biometrics: A survey," *ACM Computing Surveys*, vol. 56, no. 6, pp. 1–33, 2024, doi: [10.1145/3640311](https://doi.org/10.1145/3640311).

**Summary:** A comprehensive survey of mouse-dynamics and widget-interaction literature from 1897 to 2023. It covers psychological perspectives, data-collection tasks and settings, a taxonomy of raw attributes, **feature extraction with mathematical definitions**, public datasets, algorithms (statistical, machine learning, deep learning), fusion, performance, and limitations.

**Key findings:** It consolidates the field's features and their formal definitions, and lists open challenges and research opportunities.

**Relevance to the study:** This is the main reference for the thesis's feature and matching survey, which the adviser requested on 2026-08-28. Its catalog of feature definitions is the baseline against which the thesis positions its closed-form descriptors: chord deviation, Menger curvature, straightness ratio, and convex hull.

## 15. Is Mouse Dynamics Information Credible for User Behavior Research? An Empirical Investigation (2024)

**Reference:** E. Kuric, P. Demcak, M. Krajcovic, and P. Nemcek, "Is mouse dynamics information credible for user behavior research? An empirical investigation," *Computer Standards & Interfaces*, vol. 90, Art. 103849, 2024, doi: [10.1016/j.csi.2024.103849](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.csi.2024.103849).

**Summary:** In a controlled study with 32 participants doing three kinds of mouse activity under different mouse configurations, the authors test how much device parameters distort common mouse-dynamics metrics.

**Key findings:** Of 108 metrics evaluated, **95 and 84 were significantly affected** by mouse parameters in the two studies. Model performance can also be distorted by those parameters.

**Relevance to the study:** This is an important **threat to validity**. Enrollment and verification need the same device and pointer settings, or the descriptors need to be robust to them. It supports recording the hardware and OS pointer settings in the thesis's data protocol, and preferring shape-based descriptors (such as the straightness ratio, which doesn't depend on scale) over raw speed-based ones.

## 16. Optimizing Mouse Dynamics for User Authentication by Machine Learning: LT-AMouse (2025)

**Reference:** Y. Wang, C. Wu, Y. Liao, and M. You, "Optimizing mouse dynamics for user authentication by machine learning: Addressing data sufficiency, accuracy-practicality trade-off, and model performance challenges," *arXiv preprint* arXiv:2504.21415, 2025, doi: [10.48550/arXiv.2504.21415](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2504.21415).

**Summary:** The authors use Gaussian kernel density estimation and KL divergence to estimate how much training data is enough. They introduce the **Mouse Authentication Unit (MAU)**, which uses Approximate Entropy (ApEn) to choose an efficient segment length. Their LT-AMouse framework combines a 1D-ResNet for local features with a GRU for long-term dependencies.

**Key findings:** Training data needed was cut by about 10× on the DFL dataset. Defense AUC against blind attacks was **98.52% on DFL** and **94.65% on Balabit**.

**Relevance to the study:** This directly addresses the thesis's open question of **how long a "short signal" should be**: it picks segment length from an entropy criterion. The thesis instead segments by natural stroke boundaries (clicks, pauses, sharp reversals). The MAU approach is a principled alternative to compare against. This is a preprint.

## 17. User Identity Authentication via Spatiotemporal Mouse Dynamics Modeling (2026)

**Reference:** X. Tao, Y. Huang, J. Liu, T. Wang, W. Zhao, C. Wang, and J. Fu, "User identity authentication via spatiotemporal mouse dynamics modeling," *Computer Networks*, vol. 287, Art. 112502, 2026, doi: [10.1016/j.comnet.2026.112502](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.comnet.2026.112502).

**Summary:** The authors model mouse dynamics as a combined **spatial and temporal** problem for user identity authentication. *No public abstract could be retrieved from OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, or Crossref.* A preprint is listed at SSRN (doi: 10.2139/ssrn.6193851). Read the full text before citing any architecture or performance figures.

**Relevance to the study:** This is recent (2026) evidence that separating spatial structure from temporal behavior is an active direction in the field. That fits the thesis's emphasis on the spatial shape of strokes.

## 18. Mathematical Feature Representations of Mouse Dynamics for Continuous User Authentication (2026)

**Reference:** K. Asgarov, "Mathematical feature representations of mouse dynamics for continuous user authentication," *Problems of Information Technology*, vol. 17, no. 2, pp. 13–27, 2026, doi: [10.25045/jpit.v17.i2.02](https://doi.org/10.25045/jpit.v17.i2.02).

**Summary:** Three families of mathematical features are proposed: **path signatures** from rough path theory, **differential-geometric descriptors** of cursor trajectories, and **optimal-control residuals** based on models of human motor control. They are evaluated on Balabit and SapiMouse against a 142-feature reference set taken from the literature, all under the same one-class Mahalanobis protocol.

**Key findings:** Optimal-control features gave the best compact representation, competitive with **only 23 features**. Fusing feature families improved accuracy further. Cross-dataset tests showed that robustness between **long and short session regimes** matters.

**Relevance to the study:** This is the closest in philosophy to the thesis: explicit mathematical descriptors of trajectory geometry, compared under a controlled, one-class protocol. The main difference is matching. Mahalanobis distance compares feature *vectors*, while the thesis compares stroke *shapes* point by point with the Fréchet distance.

## 19. A Joint Patch-Based Siamese CNN with Vision Transformer Framework for Continuous Mouse Dynamics User Authentication Using Gramian Angular Summation Field (2026)

**Reference:** K. Mazumdar and S. Sundaram, "A joint patch-based Siamese CNN with vision transformer framework for continuous mouse dynamics user authentication using Gramian angular summation field," *IEEE Transactions on Biometrics, Behavior, and Identity Science*, early access, 2026, doi: [10.1109/TBIOM.2026.3740256](https://doi.org/10.1109/TBIOM.2026.3740256).

**Summary:** Mouse velocity information is encoded as **Gramian Angular Summation Field (GASF)** images, which avoid the threshold that recurrence plots require. Image patches vary a lot within a single user, so the authors train a Siamese CNN jointly with a Vision Transformer and add a patch-selection strategy that also eases class imbalance.

**Key findings:** The approach is shown to work on three public benchmark datasets.

**Relevance to the study:** This is the deep-learning state of the art, and its similarity-based (Siamese) learning echoes the thesis's template comparison. The authors note **high intra-user variability** and depend on heavy architectures. The thesis targets the same intra-user variability with a lightweight, explainable descriptor-plus-threshold design.

## 20. How Effective Is Mouse Dynamics Against Web Bots? A Study Across Different Sophistication Levels (2026)

**Reference:** O. Salman and K. Biçakcı, "How effective is mouse dynamics against web bots? A study across different sophistication levels," *IEEE Access*, vol. 14, pp. 57895–57909, 2026, doi: [10.1109/ACCESS.2026.3681065](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2026.3681065).

**Summary:** The paper evaluates three defensive layers against attacks ranging from synthetic generation to sophisticated replays: supervised ML models, **Dynamic Time Warping (DTW)** for comparing against history, and click-pattern analysis for context.

**Key findings:** No single technique worked everywhere. Random forest caught all synthetic attacks and most cross-domain replays, while **DTW was essential for same-domain replay attacks**. The authors call for a hybrid, defense-in-depth design.

**Relevance to the study:** It confirms that **direct trajectory-to-trajectory comparison** catches threats that classifiers miss. It also uses DTW, the elastic-alignment method the thesis contrasts with the discrete Fréchet distance. Fréchet keeps point order without stretching time, so it can't line up two differently shaped curves just because their timing matches.

## Synthesis

### What the 2021–2026 literature establishes

1. **Short signals are enough to identify users.** Fifteen seconds of data (Antal et al., 2021), one movement or point-and-click action (Almalki et al., 2021), or one trajectory (Acien et al., 2022; Khan & Hou, 2023) already separates users or separates humans from bots. This supports the adviser's direction to compare *short mouse signals*.
2. **Individual mannerisms persist but vary.** Different users don't produce look-alike trajectories, yet a user's own repeats also differ (Sadeghpour & Vlajic, 2023), and in image representations that within-user variation is high (Mazumdar & Sundaram, 2026). Matching therefore needs a calibrated tolerance, not an exact-match rule.
3. **Accumulating evidence across strokes gives reliable decisions.** Fusing consecutive single-trajectory scores reached 0% EER after 11 decisions (Khan & Hou, 2023). That supports the thesis's per-action trust score.
4. **The field mostly relies on opaque models.** CNNs, ResNets, GRUs, and Transformers dominate (Siddiqui et al., 2022; Durmaz & Keskinöz, 2023; Wang et al., 2025; Mazumdar & Sundaram, 2026). Explainability is added after the fact with Grad-CAM, if at all (Ciaramella et al., 2022).
5. **Closed-form mathematical features are re-emerging.** Asgarov (2026) shows compact, mathematically defined trajectory features can compete. The ACM survey (Khan et al., 2024) catalogs the formal feature definitions available.
6. **Comparing trajectories directly is valuable but rarely used.** Similarity embeddings (Jin et al., 2023) and DTW (Salman & Biçakcı, 2026) show that comparing a stroke against an enrolled sample works and catches what classifiers miss.
7. **Validity threats are documented.** Device and pointer settings change most mouse-dynamics metrics (Kuric et al., 2024). The task or scene shifts behavior (Wang et al., 2022; Siddiqui et al., 2021). Generative attacks can forge mouse actions (Roy et al., 2023; Acien et al., 2022).

### Research gap addressed by the thesis

No 2021–2026 study reviewed here combines **closed-form local geometric descriptors** of a single stroke (orthogonal chord deviation, discrete Menger curvature, straightness ratio, plus convex-hull bounding) with **discrete Fréchet distance** to compare variable-length short strokes against an enrolled profile, all feeding a **traceable per-action trust score**. The work that comes closest either:

- uses mathematical features but compares feature *vectors*, not curve shapes (Asgarov, 2026);
- compares whole trajectories but with a learned embedding (Jin et al., 2023) or time-elastic DTW (Salman & Biçakcı, 2026; Sadeghpour & Vlajic, 2023); or
- reaches high accuracy with deep models whose decisions can't be traced to a physical movement property (Wang et al., 2025; Mazumdar & Sundaram, 2026; Durmaz & Keskinöz, 2023).
